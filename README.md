## Business Problem

Bjerk Builders needed a clearer view of how opportunities moved through the pre-award journey. Most stages did not have standardized timing data, which made it difficult to see where work was waiting, address delays early, or recognize margin risk before key pricing and commitment decisions were finalized.

The project focused on a practical question: how could Bjerk improve the speed and visibility of the journey while strengthening the decisions that shape project margin?

## My Approach

I worked across the full DMAIC project lifecycle:

1. **Define:** clarified the problem, scope, customer journey, and critical-to-quality measures.
2. **Measure:** assessed data reliability and established baseline financial and operational performance.
3. **Analyze:** examined cycle-time concentration, process capability, margin drivers, failure risks, and root causes.
4. **Improve:** developed and prioritized recommendations across process flow, margin discipline, and visibility.
5. **Control:** outlined a phased roadmap and measures needed to track adoption and performance over time.

The analysis used aggregated historical operational data from 2024 to 2026. No raw client data or identifiable project-level records are included in this portfolio.

## What the Analysis Found

### A stable process can still produce unacceptable outcomes

The process capability analysis covered 428 completed projects.

- **23.4%** finished below the 10% margin target.
- **9.8%** finished below breakeven.
- Current capability was approximately **2.23 sigma**.

The control analysis showed a statistically stable process after special-cause review. That did not mean the process was performing well. Under-margin projects remained a recurring part of normal performance.

![Sanitized process capability summary](visuals/02-process-capability.png)

### Most estimated lead time was concentrated in a few stages

Four of 13 modeled stages represented approximately **81% of worst-case pre-award lead time**. Owner design completion was the largest contributor, followed by budget approval, proposal-to-award, and subcontractor mobilization to project start.

Because owner design completion is externally driven, the analysis did not treat all delay as directly controllable. The improvement opportunity was to begin eligible estimating work earlier and reduce waiting within the highest-impact internal stages.

Historical stage timestamps were unavailable, so these cycle-time conclusions were based on planning ranges rather than measured historical durations.

![Sanitized Pareto analysis of pre-award lead time](visuals/03-cycle-time-pareto.png)

### A cost-commitment gap provided an early margin-risk signal

The commitment-to-estimate gap had a **Pearson correlation of -0.67** with realized margin. Projects with larger gaps between estimated and committed costs tended to finish with lower margins.

This did not establish causation, but it identified a useful leading indicator that could be monitored at award, before final project profitability was known.

### Measurement was part of the problem

Eight of nine pre-award stages lacked standardized timing. Existing forecasts also remained unchanged for all forecastable projects reviewed, limiting their usefulness as an early view of emerging risk.

The missing measurement system was therefore not just an analytical limitation. It was one of the improvement opportunities.

## Recommendations

Seven recommendations were organized into three connected priorities.

### Improve process flow

- Move routine pursue/pass and estimator-assignment decisions to an asynchronous process, with meetings reserved for exceptions.
- Pilot estimating on partial design sets when eligibility criteria are met.

### Strengthen margin discipline

- Create risk-based paths for projects of different sizes and complexity.
- Add a decision gate for subcontractor-heavy pursuits.
- Require clearer change-order scope and a minimum-margin review before submission.

### Improve process visibility

- Establish one timestamped pursuit log with stage, owner, date-in, date-out, and aging fields.
- Standardize project-type fields, live forecasting, and continuous closeout updates.

## Projected Business Impact

The financial model estimated approximately **$940,000 in annual margin recovery under the base-case scenario**, assuming zero revenue growth.

This is a modeled opportunity, not realized savings. It was based on historical completed-project performance and partial capture of identified margin leakage. Estimated cycle-time benefits were excluded from the financial projection and treated as potential additional capacity.

![Sanitized projected business-impact summary](visuals/05-projected-business-impact.png)

## Implementation Roadmap

The proposed rollout begins with the measurement and decision-making foundation:

1. **Foundation and quick wins:** pursuit log, decision rules, forecasting discipline, and a controlled partial-design estimating pilot.
2. **Margin controls:** subcontractor-commitment and change-order review gates.
3. **Structural scale-up:** broader estimating parallelization and a tiered journey based on project risk and complexity.

This sequence allows later recommendations to be measured against a more reliable baseline.
